\section{Failure Modes}
\label{sec:failure}

The numerical gains in \cref{sec:results} leave a non-trivial gap to
perfect accuracy. To understand what is left, we hand-inspected saved
samples from \code{GSM8K} and \code{TheoremQA} produced by the
\code{dapo} and \code{grpo} checkpoints. The qualitative analysis is the
basis for the failure summary in \cref{tab:failures}; the full HTML
listing is shipped at \code{analysis/failure\_cases\_dapo\_grpo.html}.

\subsection{Format vs.\ semantics}
The most striking property of the late-stage checkpoints is that the
\emph{format} failures essentially disappear:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{Final Answer:} coverage on the saved \code{GSM8K} batches
    is $1.0$ for both \code{dapo} and \code{grpo}.
  \item No role-token leakage (\eg stray \code{user:} or
    \code{assistant:} markers) was observed in the inspected outputs.
  \item Trailing-content violations are rare; when present, they are
    short polite sign-offs that the cleanliness penalty $\pTail$ caught
    during training but did not eliminate at inference.
\end{itemize}

The remaining failures are therefore mostly \emph{semantic}, which is
what we want to characterise.

\subsection{A four-way taxonomy}
\Cref{tab:failures} groups the recurring error patterns observed in the
inspected samples.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{lXX}
\toprule
\textbf{Failure type} & \textbf{Where it appears} & \textbf{What it looks like} \\
\midrule
\emph{semantic misread of local wording}
  & \code{GSM8K}
  & The model maps a phrase to the wrong operation, \eg reading an
    alternating discount as a geometric decay. \\
\emph{event-boundary state error}
  & \code{GSM8K}
  & The model loses track of what gets reset after an interruption (a
    stop, a refill, a new day) and continues from the wrong state. \\
\emph{overconfident heuristic shortcut}
  & \code{TheoremQA}
  & A familiar theorem or symmetry is applied without checking whether the
    preconditions actually hold for the current problem. \\
\emph{clean format, wrong content}
  & \code{dapo}, \code{grpo}
  & The answer line is well-formed and the reasoning is fluent, but the
    underlying mathematical path is incorrect. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Four recurring failure patterns observed on saved samples after
RL training.}
\label{tab:failures}
\end{table}

\subsection{Examples}
\begin{example}[GSM8K, semantic misread]
A discount of \emph{50\% off, then 30\% more off the discounted price} is
sometimes read as a single 80\% discount (\ie additive) or as a
geometric decay over many steps. The model writes a clean
\code{Final Answer:} line, but the multiplicative chain
$0.5 \times 0.7$ is dropped.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[GSM8K, event-boundary error]
``She rests for one hour, then continues at the original pace'' is
treated as if the rest period also accumulates work, leading to a final
quantity that is off by exactly the rest interval.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[TheoremQA, heuristic shortcut]
A symmetry argument that holds for \emph{spherically} symmetric inputs is
applied to a \emph{cylindrically} symmetric one, producing a final
expression that is dimensionally correct but quantitatively wrong.
\end{example}

\subsection{Implications for future work}
Two practical implications follow:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Adding a \emph{precondition-check} reward bonus (or a chain-of-thought
    verifier in the reward function) could specifically attack the
    \emph{overconfident heuristic shortcut} class on \code{TheoremQA}.
  \item Adding a \emph{state-consistency} micro-reward (\eg
    automatic re-evaluation of intermediate quantities) could attack the
    \emph{event-boundary} class on \code{GSM8K}.
\end{itemize}

Both of these would require an external verifier beyond the current
final-answer check, and are left as future work.
